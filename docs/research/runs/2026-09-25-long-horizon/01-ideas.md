The initial conservative slate contains **no direction that satisfies all three conditions: searched, feasible, and value strictly greater than 1000**. We do not raise scores to fill a shortlist. D01 is the strongest bounded diagnostic for the user's explicit request to extend the time horizon and carry out theory → Lean → code. It is therefore continued as a **user-directed feasibility investigation**, not described as a high-value winner under the rubric. A known bounded voting estimator supplies an attributed long-time comparator; reproducing its all-horizon boundedness is not a novelty claim.

## What the literature changes

The raw coding-tree representation is not the only way to represent Allen–Cahn. Bounded majority/voting constructions already bypass its variance explosion by changing the representation. Restarting, local polynomial/Picard approximation, nonexponential clocks, nested branching and renormalization also have substantial precedent. This makes a generic claim such as “we can handle longer maturities” too weak a contribution description.

The useful project question is more precise: **which long-time failures can sampling changes repair, and which require a different expansion?** The previous run's exact L2 boundary concerned one family of exponential clocks and static tuple probabilities. D01 seeks an exact absolute-moment boundary for the unchanged raw expansion, which would constrain every supported proposal in its theorem's scope. The general density-cancellation argument is standard; the proposed new project result is the explicit raw flat reduction, root divergence, certified threshold and correspondence to the implementation.

## Full direction-card reading guide

The corresponding JSON records are complete and machine readable. These short descriptions explain why the directions are distinct, rather than ten parameter variants of one optimizer.

1. **D01 — a necessary boundary.** Establish the exact absolute-moment clock for the unchanged raw flat tree. This is a diagnosis of what no supported reweighting can accomplish. Its first check reconstructs the code coefficients; its success condition is an iff threshold and root divergence, not a better sample-variance plot. If the universal proposal step fails, the missing hypothesis remains explicit.

2. **D02 — a different bounded representation and its work tradeoff.** The comparator itself is prior art. The narrower question is how the continuous conditional-vote second moment changes with an adjustable event rate and whether that change is worth its extra nodes. The first falsification test challenges the tempting but unsupported assertion that the minimum admissible rate also minimizes variance times work.

3. **D03 — global approximation error.** Compose certified short slabs and account for continuation bias, derivative approximation and sampling error. Exact continuation on two slabs is the initial control, followed by a known deterministic perturbation. The unresolved dependency is a globally valid continuation representation; clipping or reusing noisy products cannot be silently called unbiased.

4. **D04 — adaptive sampling inside one expansion.** Extend lambda from one common scalar to a code/time policy with the appropriate integrated hazard. A two-code, two-rate flat example comes before time dependence. The policy proof must handle survival and future moments jointly; it cannot reuse a frozen-continuation formula as a full-tree theorem.

5. **D05 — nonconstant tuple improvement.** Develop the missing certified continuation-product bounds to make the prior safe policy gate materially useful beyond T=.05. The named target is at least a 10% certified root variance gain against the existing terminal proxy at the same rate. A rejected gate or failed upper envelope remains inconclusive.

6. **D06 — a waiting-time family comparison.** Study how much the exponential assumption costs before the raw absolute ceiling. This is largely an implementation and validation study of established constructions. A tractable scalar renewal equation is the first check because the existing ODE certificate does not automatically extend to nonmemoryless clocks.

7. **D07 — change the expansion by analytic regrouping.** Absorb an exactly known linear contribution and branch on the residual. This can potentially change total variation, unlike D04/D06. The obstacle is a valid regrouping of the representation and code system; one may not exchange divergent sums merely because formal algebra cancels terms.

8. **D08 — center around a deterministic reference.** Estimate a nonzero residual around a computable approximate solution and retain its forcing exactly. A deliberately imperfect analytic reference prevents a trivial exact-solution control from overstating success. Small value residuals must be supplemented by the derivative bounds required by the coded tree, and reference construction counts toward cost.

9. **D09 — change the iterative approximation architecture.** Reproduce multilevel Picard as a semilinear long-time baseline with explicit bias and work. This is useful comparative infrastructure with novelty 1. The first example should satisfy the published theorem; raw cubic coefficients on the whole real line do not automatically satisfy global-Lipschitz assumptions.

10. **D10 — certify a spatial boundary.** Pair positive subsolutions proving divergence with supersolutions proving finiteness for the nonconstant moment system. A spatially averaged ODE or exploding descendant does not prove root divergence at a finite point. The first test is an analytic invariant subsolution cone; failure leaves a one-sided result, not an inferred threshold.

## Bounded execution plan for the user-directed continuation

**D01 target and proof attack.** On the fixed completed raw tree space, show that the extended absolute first moment is unchanged by each admissible supported proposal. For phi=1/2, identify the least nonnegative moment recursion with the positive four-code ODE. Introduce the accumulated variable s, integrate the triangular equations, and prove the root moment is 1/2+s. Separation of the positive cubic determines the finite interval, including divergence at its endpoint. Compare this result with C8's rate-dependent second-moment boundary; they answer different questions.

**Decisive numerical check, after Lean as requested.** Check the cubic coefficients independently, enclose the positive reciprocal integral, and compare against a second independent scalar evaluation. Produce the old L2 feasible region using its existing certified C, without treating the L1 boundary as an L2 boundary. No Monte Carlo outlier pattern is evidence for the theorem.

**Lean scope.** Formalize fixed algebra, positive order/iteration facts and any deterministic reduction actually supported by available mathlib. The completed-tree measure change, ODE maximal existence, improper integral and PDE correspondence must stay outside the formal-coverage claim unless their exact declarations are checked. A green algebra build does not certify stochastic integrability or historical novelty.

**Known comparator.** Implement the attributed bounded ternary rule within the existing solver/Curve contract for bounded Allen–Cahn terminal data. Check the actual diffusion scaling and mean identity; account for every root, all nodes and failed workload limits. Expected exponential work limits the practical horizon even though the output is bounded. Any rate above the sharp boundedness threshold is an estimator choice, not a proved optimum.

**Exit conditions and cost.** Success is an exact explanatory boundary and a working, honestly attributed nonconstant comparator. The first stage needs one to three focused proof/formalization cycles and cheap scalar checks; stochastic experiments require a predeclared finite node budget because work grows exponentially. If least-solution/root divergence or correspondence fails review, report the remaining obligation and retain the counterexample. Do not weaken the claim invisibly to obtain a pass.

The strict high-value shortlist remains empty. D03, D04, D05 and D10 are plausible continuations after their named missing bounds are supplied; D02, D07 and D08 also need a tighter literature comparison. This ordering is a practical dependency assessment, not a replacement for the multiplication rubric.
