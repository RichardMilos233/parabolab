## Relevant primary sources and precise limits of this inspection

**Petras--Ritter, parabolic information complexity.** The accessible
[Dagstuhl primary record and linked paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.04401.10)
study linear parabolic equations with variable coefficients and fixed
initial data, relating complexity to integration and approximation.
The journal abstract also describes randomized variance reduction.
This is important antecedent methodology. Its stated unknown information
differs from D29's variable initial profile and fixed nonlinear equation.
The record/abstract were read; the linked 24-page PDF was opened, not fully
audited. No assertion that its every theorem excludes D29 is made.

**Lanthaler--Mishra--Karniadakis, DeepONet error estimates.** I inspected
[the authors' arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.09618), Section 4.3,
especially equations (4.31), (4.35)--(4.37), Theorem 4.27 and Theorem 4.33
(PDF pages 49--54). This includes the Allen--Cahn initial-to-final map,
finite-difference emulation and network approximation estimates. The
displayed input law uses bounded independent Fourier coefficients with
exponential decay; the concrete discussion uses spatial dimensions two
and three. The map's Lipschitz estimate explicitly depends on T. These
inspected results do not give the same growing-T minimax exact-query
statement over D29's fixed C^s class. Network size, average operator error
and unknown-input query complexity must not be conflated. This comparison
does not audit the whole 123-page paper or the later literature.

**Venturi--Dektor, nonlinear functional approximation.** I read the
abstract, introduction and derivative discussion of
[the primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40687-021-00265-4).
It establishes cylindrical approximation results for nonlinear functionals
and derivatives on compact subsets of suitable function spaces. This is
relevant approximation background. It does not license inferring a signed
product measure for every higher multilinear derivative from the ordinary
first-derivative representation. T65 proves its actual kernel construction
separately. I did not audit the full article or assert a priority exclusion.

**Chen--Villa--Ghattas, Taylor control variates.** The primary
[arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04301) and publisher result confirm
the established use of Taylor expansion, Hessian information and Monte
Carlo correction for PDE-constrained uncertainty problems. R08 already
inspected the relevant construction more closely. Taylor correction itself
is not claimed new here; neither that source nor this supplemental search
supplies D29's worst-input long-time oracle theorem or T70's desired work
bound.

## Research consequence

The potentially distinguishing statement remains the exact pair of
matching growing-T bounds on a fixed signed initial class, together with
the actual stable-interface construction and finite-query implementation.
Each ingredient has substantial antecedents. The current search supports
careful positioning and more targeted review; it does not justify a
publication-priority, major-breakthrough or award-level claim.

The next technical priority remains arithmetic efficiency. Even if D29's
query exponent is new, a construction with enormous coefficient tables is
not evidence of practical superiority. T69 and T70 investigate the two
separate derivative and known-base computation gates without importing
an efficient-work theorem from any source listed above.
