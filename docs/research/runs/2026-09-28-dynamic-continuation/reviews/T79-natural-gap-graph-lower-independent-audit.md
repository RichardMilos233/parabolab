## 2. Equation, function space and regularity

Let X=(R/Z)^d have probability Haar measure, d>=1 fixed. Write Pi for
spatial averaging, Q=I-Pi, and P_t for heat flow generated by
(kappa/2) Delta. The actual equation is

    u_t=(kappa/2) Delta u+u-u^3,
    lambda=2 pi^2 kappa>1,  nu=lambda-1>0,
    0<sigma<nu.

The constants may depend on d,kappa,sigma. Nothing below is uniform as
nu decreases to zero. This is not the heat equation with generator
Delta/2 unless kappa=1.

For continuous real data with values in [-1,1], the heat mild equation
and the locally Lipschitz cubic give local existence and uniqueness in
C(X). Comparison with the stationary constants -1 and 1 gives a uniform
bound and continues the solution for all positive times. Heat smoothing
gives classical positive-time solutions; convergence to the initial
datum is in C(X). Comparison involving continuous initial data follows
from this mild flow or by smoothing approximation and continuous
dependence. No differentiability of an arbitrary datum at time zero is
required by the energy or comparison arguments below.

For any two such solutions, their difference d solves the actual linear
equation with coefficient

    c=1-(u_1^2+u_1 u_2+u_2^2),   -2<=c<=1.

The upper inequality follows because the quadratic sum is nonnegative;
the lower inequality uses the invariant interval. In particular

    |S_t v-S_t g|<=exp(t) P_t |v-g|,
    ||S_t v-S_t g||inf<=exp(t)||v-g||inf.           (2.1)

The pointwise bound follows by comparing d and -d with the positive
supersolution exp(t)P_t|v-g|. It does not replace the reaction's absolute
Lipschitz constant by one.

## 3. The centered heat estimate is valid at all times

The Fourier multiplier of P_t is exp(-lambda |n|^2 t), n in Z^d.
Set

    rho=((1+exp(-lambda))/(1-exp(-lambda)))^d,
    M=2 rho exp(lambda),  B_t=exp(t)P_t Q.

Here the signed-kernel total variation norm is the full variation mass.
For 0<=t<=1, the kernel exp(t)(P_t(x,dy)-dy) has variation at most
2exp(t). Since 2exp(lambda t)<=2exp(lambda)<=M, this is at most
M exp(-nu t). At t=0 the kernel is the finite signed measure
delta_x-dy; no density or operator-norm heat continuity at zero is used.

For t>=1, absolute convergence of the Fourier series gives

    integral |p_t-1|
      <=sum_{n!=0} exp(-lambda |n|^2 t)
      <=exp(-lambda(t-1)) sum_{n!=0}exp(-lambda |n|^2).

The inequality |n|^2>=|n|_1 for integer coordinates implies

    sum_n exp(-lambda |n|^2)
      <=product_{i=1}^d sum_{m in Z}exp(-lambda |m|)=rho.

After multiplication by exp(t), the late-time coefficient is
(rho-1)exp(lambda)<=M. Thus exactly the candidate estimate holds:

    sup_x ||B_t(x,.)||TV<=M exp(-nu t),   t>=0.    (3.1)

The only spectral restriction used is nu>0. No estimate from the old
global-phase proof, including its stronger derivative integrability
restriction, enters this calculation.

## 4. Fixed point, actual PDE identification and graph regularity

Let E_sigma consist of continuous C(X)-valued trajectories with norm

    ||U||_sigma=sup_{t>=0} exp(sigma t)||U(t)||inf.

Multiplication by exp(sigma t) identifies this with a Banach space of
bounded continuous trajectories. Define

    (K F)(t)=-integral_0^t B_{t-a}F(a) da
             +integral_t^infinity exp(t-a)Pi F(a) da.

Both are Bochner integrals. Estimate (3.1) bounds the weighted forward
integral by M/(nu-sigma) times ||F||_sigma, and the backward scalar
integral by 1/(1+sigma) times that norm. Hence

    ||K||<=C:=M/(nu-sigma)+1/(1+sigma).            (4.1)

Strong continuity of heat flow, continuity of F, and these integrable
majorants give continuity of K F in time, including at zero. The cubic
is a bounded polynomial on E_sigma: the trilinear product has norm at
most one because exp(sigma t)exp(-3sigma t)<=1.

Fix R>0 and set

    r=R/(4M),   B=R^3/(1+3sigma),
    E=3R(1+R)/sigma+3R^2/(2sigma),
    D=2exp(E)B.

Choose R subject to the full set of candidate conditions:

    R<=1,  r<=1/8,  C R^2<=1/8,  B<=r/8,
    D<=r/16,
    R+exp(E)-1+(1-r^2)^(-1/2)-1<=1/32.

All these conditions are compatible. In fact r is a fixed positive
multiple of R, C R^2 tends to zero, B/r and D/r are O(R^2), and

    R+exp(E)-1+(1-r^2)^(-1/2)-1

tends to zero as R tends to zero. Also r<1 eventually. Thus a common
positive R, including a sufficiently small rational R, satisfies all
the displayed inequalities. No input profile enters this choice.

For mean-zero w with ||w||inf<3r, the map

    U -> B_. w+K(U^3)

maps the closed radius-R ball into the radius-7R/8 ball: its linear
part is at most 3Mr=3R/4 and its cubic part at most C R^3<=R/8.
Its Lipschitz constant there is at most 3C R^2<=3/8. Consequently
it has a unique fixed point U_w in that ball.

Taking the mean and centered component separately gives

    Q U_w(t)=B_t w-integral_0^t B_{t-a}U_w(a)^3 da,
    Pi U_w(t)=integral_t^infinity exp(t-a)Pi U_w(a)^3 da.

Define Theta(w)=Pi U_w(0). Then

    |Theta(w)|<=B<=r/8,
    U_w(0)=w+Theta(w).

Splitting the scalar integral at t gives

    Pi U_w(t)=exp(t)Theta(w)
                 -integral_0^t exp(t-a)Pi U_w(a)^3 da.

Adding this to the centered identity proves the actual mild equation

    U_w(t)=exp(t)P_t(w+Theta(w))
              -integral_0^t exp(t-a)P_{t-a}U_w(a)^3 da.

Its initial norm is below 3r+r/8<=25/64<1. Actual mild uniqueness
therefore identifies it with S_t(w+Theta(w)) for all t, with

    ||S_t(w+Theta(w))||inf<=R exp(-sigma t).       (4.2)

This supplies the very same actual stable trajectory required by D34.
There is no backward parabolic evolution; the backward integral is
solely an ordinary scalar mean identity.

Sign reversal and spatial translations commute with the fixed-point
equation. Uniqueness makes Theta odd and translation invariant. The
implicit map U-B_.w-K(U^3) has U derivative I-K(3U^2 .), whose inverse
has norm at most (1-3/8)^(-1)=8/5. Its bounded polynomial derivatives
and the uniform margin ||U_w||_sigma<=7R/8<R justify the Banach-space
implicit theorem on the entire open mean-zero 3r ball. Differentiating
the equation inductively gives uniform bounds for every fixed derivative
order: each right-hand side consists of finitely many products of
already bounded derivatives, followed by K and this uniformly bounded
inverse. No compactness of a C(X) ball is assumed.

At zero, U_0=0 and DU_0[h](t)=B_t h. Therefore DTheta(0)=0;
oddness also gives D^2Theta(0)=0.

The usual local graph invariance is consistent with this construction:
if w_t=Q U_w(t) is in the 3r domain, the shifted trajectory U_w(t+.)
has E_sigma norm at most R exp(-sigma t), satisfies the same two
integral identities with centered initial value w_t, and uniqueness
gives Pi U_w(t)=Theta(w_t). The proof does not need, or claim, that
the entire parameter ball ||w||<3r is forward invariant, or that it
classifies every possible globally decaying solution.

## 5. Full-ball variance decay and uniform sup-norm burn-in

Let ||q||inf<=a0=7/8, u=S_tq, m=Pi u and w=u-m. For positive t,
integration by parts and Pi w=0 give exactly

    d||w||2^2/dt
      =-kappa||grad u||2^2+2||w||2^2
         -2 integral (u-m)(u^3-m^3).

The last integrand is nonnegative, since the cube is increasing.
Period-one Poincare gives ||grad u||2^2>=4pi^2||w||2^2, so

    d||w||2^2/dt<=-2nu||w||2^2.

Apply this on [epsilon,t] and let epsilon decrease to zero. Sup-norm
continuity of the solution gives convergence in L2 as well, and
||Qq||2<=||q||2<=a0. Hence

    ||Q S_tq||2<=a0 exp(-nu t),   t>=0.           (5.1)

For t>=1 put a=t-1. Compare u(a+h) with the spatially constant actual
solution b(h) whose initial value is Pi u(a). Its initial value lies
in [-1,1], so both solutions have that invariant bound. Their
difference has coefficient c<=1 as in (2.1), and consequently

    |u(a+1)-b(1)|<=e P_1|Q u(a)|.

Cauchy--Schwarz against the actual heat density gives a sup bound
e||p_1||2||Q u(a)||2. Subtracting its mean costs at most two:

    ||Q S_tq||inf<=2e||p_1||2 a0 exp(-nu(t-1)).

Parseval and the sum estimate from Section 3 imply

    ||p_1||2^2=sum_n exp(-2lambda |n|^2)<=rho,
    ||p_1||2<=sqrt(rho)<=rho.

Dropping a0<=1 leaves precisely the candidate's uniform bound

    ||Q S_tq||inf<=Cw exp(-nu t),
    Cw=2e rho exp(nu),   t>=1.                    (5.2)

This is an actual linear comparison between two solutions. It does not
treat Q as positive or appeal to a maximum principle for a spectral
discretization. Its constants use no derivative of q and therefore
cover the complete closed 7/8 sup-norm ball of continuous inputs.

For example the public integer

    L=max(1,ceil(log(64 Cw/r)/nu))

satisfies Cw exp(-nu L)<=r/64. Thus every such input enters the
strict graph domain in its centered coordinate at this single time.
Its mean need not be small. The argument properly retains that
distinction for the later branch test.

## 6. The proxy is smooth, correctly normalized and not a global phase

Define on the closed 7/8 ball

    H(q)=Pi S_Lq-Theta(Q S_Lq),
    Atilde(q)=exp(-L)H(q).

The centered argument is uniformly at most r/64<3r. On the open
7/8 ball the finite-time flow is C-infinity in C(X). This can be
seen directly by differentiating its finite-time mild equation: the
first variation has potential 1-3u^2 in [-2,1], the second and third
reaction derivatives are -6u and -6, and all higher ones vanish.
The propagator bound by exp(t-a)P_{t-a} and induction on the finite
partitions of derivative labels give uniform finite operator bounds
at every fixed order and fixed time L. These bounds depend on L,
not on spatial derivatives of the datum. The same equations justify
continuous dependence of the derivatives on the datum.

Combining these bounds with Section 4 proves H is C-infinity on an
open neighborhood of the closed 3/4 ball, with the asserted uniform
fixed-order bounds. H is odd. Since DS_L(0)=exp(L)P_L and
DTheta(0)=0,

    D Atilde(0)=Pi,   D^2 Atilde(0)=0.             (6.1)

These are ordinary multilinear operator estimates on C(X). They do
not establish a marked L1 direction bound, derivative measures, a
sampler, or a global phase identity such as H(S_tq)=exp(t)H(q).
None of those stronger properties is used for R14c's output proof.

## 7. Exact local/far buffers and the paid-data boundary

The full-ball analytical statement can be expressed cleanly as follows.
For any ||q||inf<=7/8, let b=Pi S_Lq, w=Q S_Lq, and suppose a scalar
mhat obeys |mhat-b|<=r/16. If |mhat|<=r/2, then

    |b|<=9r/16,   ||w||inf<=r/64,
    |b-Theta(w)|<=11r/16<r.

The actual initial datum w+b=S_Lq lies in [-1,1]. The constructed
trajectory S_t(w+Theta(w)) obeys (4.2), with the backward mean
identity already proved in Section 4. All hypotheses of D34 therefore
hold with h=b-Theta(w) and h_max=r<1. D34 bounds the output error by
R+exp(E)-1+(1-r^2)^(-1/2)-1, and the radius choice gives

    |S_{L+t}q(x)-Psi(exp(t)H(q))|<=1/32,
    Psi(z)=z/sqrt(1+z^2),   t>=0.                 (7.1)

This includes h=0 and arbitrarily small shifts of either sign.
No lower bound on |H(q)| or phase multiplier is needed.

If |mhat|>r/2, put sgn=sign(mhat). Then

    sgn*b>7r/16,
    sgn*S_Lq(x)>7r/16-r/64=27r/64>=r/4.

Oddness and comparison place the signed future solution between
phi_t(r/4) and 1, where

    phi_t(a)=a exp(t)/sqrt(1+a^2(exp(2t)-1)).

For a=r/4 in (0,1),

    phi_t(a)=[1+(a^(-2)-1)exp(-2t)]^(-1/2).

It increases to one. For example any nonnegative t_far with

    t_far>=0.5 log((225/31)(16/r^2-1))

ensures phi_t(r/4)>=15/16 for all t>=t_far. Thus returning sgn has
absolute error at most 1/16 at every T>=L+t_far, with the same
deterministic guarantee for every allowed mean error.

The displayed paid-g interface in R14c is narrower than this analytical
full-ball statement. It assumes an unknown ||v||inf<=1/2, a supplied
known approximation ||g||inf<=3/4 with ||v-g||inf<=delta and
exp(L)delta<=r/32, and a finite certified mean computation satisfying

    |mhat-Pi S_Lg|<=r/32.

Equation (2.1) gives |Pi S_Lv-Pi S_Lg|<=r/32; summing the two
errors yields exactly |mhat-Pi S_Lv|<=r/16. This proves all of its
stated branch constants. Equality at |mhat|=r/2 belongs to the safe
near branch; the far branch's sign inequalities are strict.

R14c does not construct g, charge an interpolation scheme, or supply
the certified mean evaluator. In particular the specific promise
||g||<=3/4 with tiny residual is not asserted to be available for
every q in the larger 7/8 ball. Uniform full-ball burn-in and the
conditional full-ball inequalities above do not create that missing
algorithmic interface. No free mean, PDE-value or unknown-data access
has been introduced by this PASS.

## 8. Graph-root interface matches accepted D34

For a supplied continuous mean-zero wtilde with ||wtilde||inf<=r/8,
the graph is defined, |Theta(wtilde)|<=r/8, and its actual stable
trajectory has the same R,sigma. The trial inputs
wtilde+c, c in [-r/4,r/4], have norm at most 3r/8<1. The chosen
D<=r/16 and r<=1/8 imply D<=min(1,r/16), exactly as D34 requires.

Consequently the actual mean function f_tau(c)=Pi S_tau(wtilde+c)
has a unique root c_tau in that bracket, with

    |c_tau-Theta(wtilde)|<=D exp(-(1+3sigma)tau),
    |c_tau|<=3r/16,
    f_tau(c2)-f_tau(c1)>=exp(-2tau)(c2-c1)

for c2>c1. The lower slope comes from the same coefficient c>=-2
in Section 2. The sharper root rate comes from D34's signed logistic
comparison to the decaying trajectory, not by dividing its mean by
that coarse lower slope. Hence no extra condition on sigma is hidden
in the root estimate.

For 0<zeta<r choose tau=max(1,ceil(log(1/zeta))). Then

    D exp(-(1+3sigma)tau)<=D exp(-tau)<=D zeta
      <=zeta/16<zeta/2.

If each mean estimate has error at most epsilon_f=exp(-2tau)zeta/8,
an estimate above epsilon_f puts the root below its midpoint; an
estimate below -epsilon_f puts it above. Otherwise the true mean
has magnitude at most 2epsilon_f, and the slope bound places the
midpoint within zeta/4 of c_tau. If no early return occurs,
N=ceil(log_2(r/zeta)) halvings reduce the initial length r/2 to at
most zeta/2. Its midpoint is then within zeta/4 of c_tau. In either
case the error from Theta(wtilde) is less than zeta.

The loop count is finite and public; all evaluations use the same
time and tolerance. An actual representation of wtilde and a finite
certified evaluator remain necessary. This is an evaluator reduction,
not a cost or existence theorem for that evaluator.

## 10. Single-cell influence and concentration on the entire layer

Put F(v)=Atilde(v)-Pi v. From (6.1), Taylor's formula for the derivative
along the segment from zero to v gives the actual identity

    DF(v)[h]=integral_0^1(1-t)D^3 Atilde(tv)[v,v,h] dt,
    |DF(v)[h]|<=(B3/2)||v||inf^2||h||1.           (10.1)

Choose psi in C_c^infinity((0,1)^d), 0<=psi<=1, I=integral psi>0,
Dpsi=max(1,max_{|alpha|<=s}||partial^alpha psi||inf), and

    a=1/(8Dpsi),  k even,  K=k^d,  Aamp=a k^(-s),
    v_xi=sum_j xi_j Aamp psi(kx-j),  xi_j in {-1,+1}.

The bump supports are disjoint. A sign flip changes the datum in L1
by exactly 2Aamp I/K, and its joining segment has sup norm at most
Aamp. Once Aamp<=a_star, integrating (10.1) along that segment gives

    |F(v_xi)-F(v_flip_i xi)|<=B3 I Aamp^3/K
       <=Lflip:=B3 Aamp^3/K.                     (10.2)

A swap costs at most 2Lflip. These bounds do not require the PDE or F
to commute with arbitrary permutations of spatial cells. Intermediate
flip profiles need only remain in the analytic sup ball, which they do.

Let pi_ell be the uniform layer with sum of signs ell, where K,ell
are even and sqrt(K)<=ell<=2sqrt(K)<=K/4. Oddness pairs opposite
configurations in the balanced layer, so E_pi0 F=0. Flipping an
ell/2-subset chosen uniformly from the negative cells of a uniform
balanced vector yields exactly pi_ell: every final positive set has
the same number of balanced predecessors, with equal pair weights.
It follows that

    |E_piell F|<=ell Lflip/2.                     (10.3)

For the variance, reveal cells in any fixed order. Given a prefix for
which both next signs are possible, the positive-suffix counts differ
by one. Couple the smaller uniform subset to the larger by adding a
uniform complement element. Equal predecessor counts prove the larger
subset is uniform. The coupled complete configurations differ by one
swap, so the two conditional expectations differ by at most 2Lflip.
With next-positive probability p, the Doob increment therefore has
conditional variance at most p(1-p)(2Lflip)^2<=Lflip^2. Forced signs
give zero increments. Orthogonality of the finite martingale gives

    Var_piell(F)<=K Lflip^2=B3^2 Aamp^6/K.         (10.4)

All random variables here have finite variance: the layer has finite
support and F is a finite real functional there. Equation (10.4) is
a bound on that actual finite-prior variance, not an iid surrogate.

The mass on pi_ell is m=Aamp I ell/K. The condition

    Aamp^2<=I/(64B3)

implies |E F|/m<=B3 Aamp^2/(2I)<=1/128<=1/8.
Thus |F|>m/4 implies |F-EF|>m/8. Chebyshev gives

    P_piell(|F|>m/4)
      <=64 B3^2 Aamp^4 K/(I^2 ell^2)
      <=64 B3^2 Aamp^4/I^2<=1/64.                (10.5)

Sign reversal gives the same estimate on pi_{-ell}. Both are the
original full layers. No conditioning on the good event is performed.

## 11. Smooth genuine signs and actual PDE separation at all large T

Put qrate=s+d/2 and, for each public real T, choose

    R_T=(a I exp(T))^(1/qrate),
    k=2 floor(R_T/2),  K=k^d,
    ell=2 ceil(sqrt(K)/2).

For R_T>=4, R_T/2<=k<=R_T. For K>=2048 the chosen ell is even and
lies in [sqrt(K),2sqrt(K)], with 2sqrt(K)<=K/4. Hence both layer
sign counts are positive integers strictly below K. Because psi has
positive integral, every member of either layer takes both strict
signs, including every exceptional member from (10.5).

Compact support away from cell boundaries gives smooth periodic
extensions. For each multi-index of total order at most s,

    ||partial^alpha v_xi||inf
      <=a Dpsi k^(|alpha|-s)<=1/8.

Also ||v_xi||inf<=Aamp<=1/8. These are members of the fixed original
class; no common bound on their higher derivatives is claimed.

An explicit all-horizon threshold is available. Choose an integer
k_*>=4 so that

    k_*^d>=2048,
    a k_*^(-s)<=min(a_star,r exp(-L)/4),
    a^2 k_*^(-2s)<=I/(64B3).

Such a k_* exists since s>=1 and all constants are fixed and positive.
Then the finite threshold

    T0=max(L,qrate log(2k_*)-log(aI))

ensures every needed condition for every real T>=T0, including between
jumps of the even floor. No subsequence or relation d<=4s remains.

The exact mass identity is

    exp(T)m=(R_T/k)^qrate ell/sqrt(K).

It gives 1<=exp(T)m<=2^(qrate+1). On the positive good event
|F|<=m/4, Atilde(v)=m+F>=3m/4, and so

    Psi(exp(T)Atilde(v))>=Psi(3/4)=3/5.

Equations (9.2) and the actual local output estimate subtract at most
1/32. Thus

    S_Tv(x*)>=3/5-1/32=91/160>1/2.               (11.1)

The negative good set has target at most -91/160. These are actual
PDE targets under diffusivity kappa, not scalar evolution of the
initial mean or evaluation of a global asymptotic phase.

## 12. Exact-value observations retain the accepted finite information law

Use the equal mixture of pi_ell and pi_{-ell}, with their full original
weights. An admissible algorithm knows only public parameters and
acquires unknown-input information through exact initial point values.
Every acquisition, including preprocessing and repeated evaluations,
is charged. Its seed is input-independent, rules and outputs are
Borel, and it halts almost surely on every promised input.

Give a simulator the sign of the half-open cell containing each
queried point, even where psi is zero. That sign and the point determine
the true queried value, so this strengthens the original oracle. No
query contains two unknown signs, because supports are disjoint and
boundary values are zero. Repeated cells need no new sign for the
simulation, while their original query costs are still counted.

For a procedure capped at n=floor(K/1024) original queries, pad its
distinct revealed cells by any public unused-cell rule to n distinct
signs. Conditional on the input-independent seed and any revealed
signs and labels, the unrevealed signs remain exchangeable under each
full layer. Adaptive choice of the next unused label therefore has
the same without-replacement law as fixed-order sampling. The actual
query locations, original values, stopping action and output label
are a common measurable function of the seed and this padded sequence.
They do not contribute additional information.

At a prefix of length j<n with z positives, the next-positive
probabilities are exactly

    p_plus=((K+ell)/2-z)/(K-j),
    p_minus=((K-ell)/2-z)/(K-j).

Every prefix of this length is possible under both laws, since the
smaller sign count is at least 3K/8>n. Using j<=K/8 and ell<=K/4,

    p_minus>=(3K/8-K/8)/K=1/4,
    p_minus<=(K/2)/(7K/8)=4/7<3/4,
    |p_plus-p_minus|=ell/(K-j)<=2ell/K.

The Bernoulli bound derived from log x<=x-1 gives

    KL(Ber(p_plus)||Ber(p_minus))
      <=(p_plus-p_minus)^2/[p_minus(1-p_minus)]
      <=(64/3)ell^2/K^2<=256/(3K).

Finite conditional chain rule then gives padded-sign KL at most
n*256/(3K)<=1/12. The seed has a common law under both hypotheses;
conditioning on it gives the same urn calculation, so adjoining it
preserves this bound. Data processing covers every capped decision.
With probability total variation distance, Pinsker gives

    TV<=sqrt(KL/2)<=sqrt(1/24)<1/4.

Thus every capped sign test has equal-prior error at least
(1-TV)/2>=3/8. This is exactly the accepted T61/T64 information
experiment. The diffusion coefficient, the coordinate used to prove
PDE separation, and the good event do not appear in it. Conditioning
on that good event would in general destroy this exchangeability;
R14d correctly never does so.

## 13. Finite risk, random stopping and expected cost

Let an algorithm satisfy the class-uniform MSE bound 1/16 for the
actual target. Threshold its real output at zero with any fixed tie
rule. On each good input, an incorrect label incurs absolute error
at least 91/160>1/2. Markov therefore bounds its error probability
by 1/4. On a bad input use the bound one. Equation (10.5) yields

    original Bayes sign error<=1/4+1/64=17/64<=9/32.

The last bound deliberately keeps the accepted T61/T64 error budget.
An unbounded or biased output causes no difficulty. In fact the MSE
premise and |S_Tv(x*)|<=1 imply E|output|^2<=17/8 on every input,
so finite output variance follows from the risk premise. No separate
uniform integrability or estimator-unbiasedness assumption is used.

Let qbar be the original expected query count averaged over this
finite prior and the seed. If qbar is infinite, the lower bound is
immediate. Otherwise truncate before original query n+1 and choose
a fixed sign there. The decision changes only on {Q>n}, which has
prior probability at most qbar/n. Consequently

    3/8<=9/32+qbar/n,
    qbar>=3n/32>=3K/65536.                       (13.1)

Here n>=K/2048 follows from K>=2048. The cap concerns all original
acquisitions, not only distinct cells. Its subsequent padding is an
information-theoretic simulation and is not counted as free input
work performed by the original estimator.

Measurable random stopping and almost-sure halting are sufficient.
For each fixed T the prior has finite support, so one may discard the
union of its inputwise null exceptional seed sets; arbitrary common
behavior there does not affect any prior probability. The truncated
decision and padding remain measurable. No answer-independent stopping
rule or deterministic bound on the original query count is imposed.

Finally k>=R_T/2 in (13.1) gives exactly

    qbar>=3*2^(-d-16)*(aI)^(d/qrate) exp(dT/qrate)
         =c exp(2dT/(2s+d)).                     (13.2)

The prior is chosen from public parameters and T independently of
the algorithm. Its average lower bound implies a worst-input expected
query lower bound; an expensive member may depend on both the horizon
and the algorithm. Any work model satisfying Work>=Q inherits this
lower inequality. No matching work upper bound, common expensive
baseline, or single hard profile for all horizons follows.
