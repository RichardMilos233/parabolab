## Primary material and its relevance

Kunsch, *Linear Monte Carlo quadrature with optimal confidence intervals*,
[author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09059), abstract/introduction
and Theorem 2.1 (PDF page 6) were inspected. It proves optimal
probabilistic integration rates for continuous isotropic Sobolev classes
using stratified control variates. For p=infinity and fixed confidence,
the exponent is s/d+1/2. This confirms that the interpolation-plus-
sampling exponent used in our work is established integration machinery;
it is not itself a new discovery. The inspected statement concerns an
integral, not the growing-time nonlinear Allen–Cahn solution map or
the cost of computing its stable-manifold phase. The confidence
criterion also differs from our fixed RMS contract. No runtime theorem
for our nonlinear solver is imported from this source.

Heinrich, *Randomized Complexity of Parametric Integration and the Role
of Adaption II. Sobolev Spaces*, [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13499),
abstract and opening problem statement were inspected. Its object is a
linear integration operator in one group of variables, with the result
approximated as a function of another group. This is relevant background
for parameter-dependent information complexity, but does not establish
the nonlinear long-time work claim under audit. The full proof was not
reviewed, and no stronger exclusion or attribution claim is made.

Heinrich, *The randomized information complexity of elliptic PDE*,
[publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0885064X05001135),
was identified through the publisher abstract and an indexed excerpt of
the author's PDF. It addresses linear elliptic boundary problems with a
smooth right-hand side, including pointwise output. Its integration-like
randomized exponent is relevant prior art. The full author PDF could not
be opened in this session, so its detailed algorithm and arithmetic
accounting have not been audited here.

The search also found Heinrich's *Multilevel Monte Carlo Methods* and its
indexed author-PDF section on Fredholm equations. Opening that PDF failed.
It remains a literature lead, not a source whose full argument was read.
The already inspected Petras–Ritter and Taylor-control-variate works in
R11 remain relevant; this note does not duplicate or upgrade those reads.

## Consequence for the research claim

T69 and T70 must be evaluated on the extra obligations beyond classical
randomized integration: an actual phase-proxy derivative sampler with
uniform second moments and charged work, and a deterministic computation
of the paid base profile at exponentially small absolute error. Their
combination would concern a growing-time nonlinear solution map in an
explicit ideal model. That difference is a candidate contribution to
assess, not a proof of originality, importance, or award-level merit.

Independent T73/T74 and root model correspondence remain necessary.
