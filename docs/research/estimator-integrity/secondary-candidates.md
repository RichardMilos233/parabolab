# Seven secondary candidates for branching-estimator integrity

This note is a claim ledger for Candidates 4--10 in the estimator-integrity
programme. Each candidate uses the same eight-part template. Its `Status`
field is the status of the candidate contribution as a whole; a complete
algebraic lemma may therefore appear inside a candidate whose broader method
is classified as prior-art overlap or conjecture.

The note distinguishes a formal mechanism from a valid stochastic
representation. A mechanism identity alone does not supply existence of a
classical solution, integrability of the random functional, nonexplosion of
the tree, or uniqueness of the infinite code-indexed mild system. Likewise,
finite Monte Carlo output does not establish a moment condition.

## Candidate 4: State-dependent arbitrary-jet representation

### Precise statement

Consider the terminal-value problem

\[
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\partial_tu(t,x)+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\Delta u(t,x)
+f\!\left(x,D^{\alpha_1}u(t,x),\ldots,D^{\alpha_m}u(t,x)\right)=0,\\[1mm]
u(T,x)=\phi(x),
\end{cases}
\qquad x\in\mathbb R^d,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where \(\alpha_p\in\mathbb N_0^d\). Write

\[
J u=(D^{\alpha_1}u,\ldots,D^{\alpha_m}u),\qquad
G_{a,\beta,\nu}
:=\left(a\,\partial_x^\beta\partial_z^\nu f\right)^* ,
\tag{4.2}
\]

with \(\beta\in\mathbb N_0^d\), \(\nu\in\mathbb N_0^m\), and
\(a\in\mathbb R\). In addition to \(\mathrm{Id}\), use spatial codes
\(D^\mu\), \(\mu\in\mathbb N_0^d\).

The spatial chain rule can be encoded without an implicit appeal to a
Faà-di-Bruno formula. An expansion record

\[
(A,\beta,\nu;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s)
\tag{4.3}
\]

represents

\[
A\,
(\partial_x^\beta\partial_z^\nu f)(x,Ju)
\prod_{j=1}^sD^{\lambda_j}u.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let \(\mathscr E_0\) contain the single record
\((1,0,0;\varnothing)\). Fix the coordinate order
\(\partial_{x_1}^{\mu_1}\cdots\partial_{x_d}^{\mu_d}\). Each application of
\(\partial_{x_k}\) replaces every record (4.3) by the following labelled
list:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(A,\beta+e_k,\nu;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s),\\
&(A,\beta,\nu+e_p;
  \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s,\alpha_p+e_k),
  &&p=1,\ldots,m,\\
&(A,\beta,\nu;
  \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_j+e_k,\ldots,\lambda_s),
  &&j=1,\ldots,s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Repeated records remain repeated labelled terms. The resulting finite list
is \(\mathscr E_\mu\), and it satisfies

\[
D^\mu[f(x,Ju)]
=
\sum_{(A,\beta,\nu;\Lambda)\in\mathscr E_\mu}
A(\partial_x^\beta\partial_z^\nu f)(x,Ju)
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}D^\lambda u.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Using labelled disjoint unions, the state-dependent mechanism is

\[
\mathcal M(\mathrm{Id})=\{(G_{1,0,0})\},
\tag{4.7}
\]

\[
\mathcal M(D^\mu)
=
\left\{
\bigl(G_{A,\beta,\nu},D^{\lambda_1},\ldots,D^{\lambda_s}\bigr):
(A,\beta,\nu;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s)\in\mathscr E_\mu
\right\},
\tag{4.8}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M(G_{a,\beta,\nu})
={}&
\biguplus_{p=1}^m
\left\{
\bigl(
G_{a,\beta,\nu+e_p},
G_{A,\beta',\nu'},
D^{\lambda_1},\ldots,D^{\lambda_s}
\bigr):
(A,\beta',\nu';\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s)
\in\mathscr E_{\alpha_p}
\right\}\\
&\uplus
\left\{
\bigl(G_{-a\sigma^2/2,\,\beta+2e_k,\,\nu}\bigr):
k=1,\ldots,d
\right\}\\
&\uplus
\left\{
\bigl(
G_{-a\sigma^2,\,\beta+e_k,\,\nu+e_p},
D^{\alpha_p+e_k}
\bigr):
p=1,\ldots,m,\ k=1,\ldots,d
\right\}\\
&\uplus
\left\{
\bigl(
G_{-a\sigma^2/2,\,\beta,\,\nu+e_p+e_q},
D^{\alpha_p+e_k},
D^{\alpha_q+e_k}
\bigr):
p,q=1,\ldots,m,\ k=1,\ldots,d
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Terms known identically zero may be pruned; a numerical zero at sampled
states is not enough to remove a labelled term.

**Conditional representation theorem.** Under the assumptions listed below,
the coding-tree functional generated by (4.7)--(4.9), with terminal factors

\[
g_{D^\mu}=D^\mu\phi,\qquad
g_{G_{a,\beta,\nu}}(x)
=a(\partial_x^\beta\partial_z^\nu f)(x,J\phi(x)),
\tag{4.10}
\]

satisfies

\[
\mathbb E H_{t,x,\mathrm{Id}}=u(t,x),\quad
\mathbb E H_{t,x,D^\mu}=D^\mu u(t,x),\quad
\mathbb E H_{t,x,G_{a,\beta,\nu}}
=a(\partial_x^\beta\partial_z^\nu f)(x,Ju(t,x)).
\tag{4.11}
\]

### Minimal assumptions

1. The coefficient \(\sigma^2\geq0\) is constant, and the Markov motion has
   generator \((\sigma^2/2)\Delta\).
2. The functions \(f\), \(\phi\), and the classical solution \(u\) have every
   spatial and jet derivative reached from the requested root codes. For a
   theorem covering all arbitrary-order roots, it is sufficient to assume
   \(f,\phi\in C^\infty\) and \(u\in C^{1,\infty}\), with the derivatives
   continuous and satisfying the required Duhamel formulas.
3. Spatial derivatives commute with
   \(\partial_t+(\sigma^2/2)\Delta\) on this solution, and differentiating
   (4.1) to every reached order is licensed.
4. Every live mechanism table is finite and is sampled with positive
   probability; branch-time and Markov likelihood ratios are exact.
5. The continuous-time multitype tree is nonexplosive almost surely.
6. Every reached tree functional is absolutely integrable. Passage from
   killed depth to the unrestricted tree requires \(L^1\) convergence, not
   only almost-sure stabilization.
7. The infinite code-indexed mild system generated by (4.7)--(4.10) has a
   unique solution in a stated class containing both the classical code
   fields and the tree expectations.

The last four requirements are independent. In particular, nonexplosion does
not imply integrability or uniqueness.

### Status

**conditional theorem**

### Proof or proof route

First, (4.6) follows by induction. Applied to (4.4),
\(\partial_{x_k}\) can hit the explicit \(x\)-argument of \(f\), one jet
argument \(D^{\alpha_p}u\), or one displayed \(u\)-factor. These three cases
are exactly the three lines in (4.5), including their multiplicities.

For the less direct code \(G_{a,\beta,\nu}\), put

\[
h(x,z)=a\,\partial_x^\beta\partial_z^\nu f(x,z),
\qquad
w(t,x)=h(x,Ju(t,x)),
\qquad
\mathcal L=\partial_t+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\Delta.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Since \(\mathcal L D^{\alpha_p}u=-D^{\alpha_p}[f(x,Ju)]\), the multivariate
chain rule gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Lw
={}&-\sum_{p=1}^m
h_{z_p}\,D^{\alpha_p}[f(x,Ju)]
+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\sum_{k=1}^d h_{x_kx_k}\\
&+\sigma^2\sum_{p=1}^m\sum_{k=1}^d
h_{x_kz_p}\,D^{\alpha_p+e_k}u\\
&+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}
\sum_{p,q=1}^m\sum_{k=1}^d
h_{z_pz_q}\,
D^{\alpha_p+e_k}u\,D^{\alpha_q+e_k}u.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.13}
\]

The source in the backward Duhamel formula for \(w\) is
\(-\mathcal Lw\). Expanding \(D^{\alpha_p}[f(x,Ju)]\) by (4.6) produces the
first line of (4.9); the remaining three lines of (4.9) are the negatives of
the final three terms in (4.13). This proves the mechanism algebra.

The classical fields on the right of (4.11) therefore solve the
code-indexed mild system. First-event conditioning shows that the killed-depth
tree expectations solve its finite Picard iterates. Absolute integrability,
nonexplosion, and the stated \(L^1\) passage identify the unrestricted tree
expectations as a solution of the same infinite system. The uniqueness
hypothesis then yields (4.11).

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

The mechanism algebra does not prove (4.11). The Dym example in
`dym-nonintegrability.md` has a valid formal coding mechanism and a finite
five-particle topology on which the absolute first moment is infinite.
State dependence adds mixed derivatives that can create further unbounded
terminal factors. Thus removing the integrability assumption is false in
general.

Uniqueness also cannot be omitted from this proof route. First-event
conditioning only shows that the expectation is a mild-system solution; it
does not select the intended classical solution if the infinite coupled
system admits more than one solution in the comparison class.

### Closest prior art

Nguwi, Penent, and Privault derive state-independent arbitrary-derivative
coding trees in *A fully nonlinear Feynman--Kac formula with derivatives of
arbitrary orders*, J. Evol. Equ. 23, 22 (2023),
[DOI 10.1007/s00028-023-00873-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00028-023-00873-3).
Their deep implementation is *A deep branching solver for fully nonlinear
partial differential equations*, J. Comput. Phys. 499, 112712 (2024),
[DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712).

Henry-Labordère, Oudjane, Tan, Touzi, and Warin allow time/space-dependent
coefficients in semilinear polynomial problems involving \(u\) and \(Du\),
using marked branching and Malliavin weights, in *Branching diffusion
representation of semilinear PDEs and Monte Carlo approximation* (2019),
[DOI 10.1214/17-AIHP880](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-AIHP880).
Huang and Privault study integrability and mild-system uniqueness for a
semilinear coded branching solver in *Stability analysis of a branching
diffusion solver for semilinear heat equations* (preprint first posted 2025),
[arXiv:2502.17853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17853).

These works make the overlap substantial. The narrower item assessed here is
the explicit mixed \(x\)-jet mechanism (4.9) for arbitrary jet orders,
together with an honest conditional representation statement. Priority for
that exact combination has not been established by this bounded audit.

### Numerical falsification test

1. For random low-degree polynomial \(f(x,z)\) and every
   \(|\mu|\leq4\), compare (4.6) symbolically with direct differentiation by
   a computer algebra system after substituting independent jet symbols.
2. Set all explicit \(x\)-derivatives of \(f\) to zero and compare the
   labelled table with the existing state-independent mechanism, retaining
   duplicate multiplicities.
3. Use manufactured state-dependent equations such as
   \(u_t+\tfrac12u_{xx}+xu=0\), \(u(T,x)=1\), for which
   \(u(t,x)=\exp(x(T-t)+(T-t)^3/6)\). Test the \(\mathrm{Id}\), \(D\), and
   \(G\) roots separately.
4. Compare killed-depth moments from the exact first-event recursion with
   Monte Carlo from the identically killed tree. A mismatch localized to the
   mixed terms falsifies (4.9); a mismatch after independently resampling
   child birth positions detects violation of the shared-position rule.

### Quant-research relevance

Explicit state dependence is required for stochastic-rate, local-volatility,
and factor-dependent HJB equations. Arbitrary-jet roots provide direct labels
for values, Greeks, and policy ingredients. The conditional qualifications
are material in finance: a formally generated Greek is not a usable risk
number unless its tree functional has the moment needed by the reported
uncertainty measure.

## Candidate 5: Derivative-code Sobolev training

### Precise statement

Let \(\mathcal A\) be a finite set of multi-indices, let
\(w_\mu\geq0\), and let \(X\sim P_X\). Suppose a derivative-root coding tree
provides a real label \(Y_\mu\) satisfying

\[
\mathbb E[Y_\mu\mid X]=D^\mu u(X).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every candidate \(v\) whose displayed derivatives are square integrable,
define

\[
\mathcal R(v)
:=
\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal A}
w_\mu\,
\mathbb E\!\left[
\bigl(Y_\mu-D^\mu v(X)\bigr)^2
\right].
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R(v)
=
\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal A}
w_\mu
\left\|D^\mu u-D^\mu v\right\|_{L^2(P_X)}^2
+
\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal A}
w_\mu\,
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(Y_\mu\mid X)].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The final sum is independent of \(v\). Hence the population minimizers over
a hypothesis class are exactly the weighted Sobolev projections of \(u\),
provided all terms in (5.3) are finite.

### Minimal assumptions

1. The index set \(\mathcal A\) is finite. A countable version requires
   summability and Tonelli conditions.
2. For every positively weighted \(\mu\),
   \(Y_\mu\in L^2\) and
   \(D^\mu u(X),D^\mu v(X)\in L^2\).
3. Equation (5.1) holds for the same distribution \(P_X\) used in the loss.
4. The network is differentiable to order
   \(\max_{\mu\in\mathcal A}|\mu|\) at \(P_X\)-almost every input.

For a multivariate central limit theorem for learned Greeks, the joint label
vector must have a finite covariance matrix. For a policy or Greek ratio
\(G(Ju)\), \(G\) must be differentiable at the target jet; any denominator
must be separated from zero, preferably
\(\inf_{x\in D}|d(Ju(x))|\geq\kappa>0\) on the reporting domain.

### Status

**prior-art overlap**

### Proof or proof route

Fix \(\mu\), write

\[
m_\mu(X):=D^\mu u(X),\qquad
\eta_\mu:=Y_\mu-m_\mu(X),\qquad
g_\mu(X):=D^\mu v(X).
\tag{5.4}
\]

By (5.1), \(\mathbb E[\eta_\mu\mid X]=0\). Expanding the square and
conditioning on \(X\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(Y_\mu-g_\mu(X))^2\mid X]
&=
\mathbb E[
(\eta_\mu+m_\mu(X)-g_\mu(X))^2
\mid X]\\
&=
\mathbb E[\eta_\mu^2\mid X]
+(m_\mu(X)-g_\mu(X))^2\\
&\quad+
2(m_\mu(X)-g_\mu(X))
\mathbb E[\eta_\mu\mid X]\\
&=
\operatorname{Var}(Y_\mu\mid X)
+(D^\mu u(X)-D^\mu v(X))^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Taking expectations in (5.5), multiplying by \(w_\mu\), and summing the
finite index set proves (5.3). This is an orthogonal-projection identity, not
a finite-sample optimization or generalization theorem. Network
approximation error, stochastic optimization error, and estimation error
remain separate.

If a joint label vector \(Z\) for the required derivatives lies in \(L^2\),
then the ordinary multivariate central limit theorem applies to independent
sample means. A differentiable map \(G\) with a nonzero denominator then
admits the delta-method covariance

\[
\frac1N\nabla G(Ju)^\top\Sigma\,\nabla G(Ju).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Neither (5.3) nor (5.6) applies when the corresponding second moments fail.

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

If \(Y_\mu\notin L^2\), the left side of (5.3) can be infinite for every
\(v\), so squared Sobolev training has no finite population risk. The Dym
derivative codes provide a concrete warning: shallow terminal singularities
can destroy even \(L^1\), despite finite-looking batches.

Ratio recovery has a separate obstruction. If a control is
\(G(z_1,z_2)=z_1/z_2\) and \(z_2\) approaches zero, small absolute jet errors
can produce unbounded policy errors. Accurate value and derivative losses do
not supply a ratio guarantee without denominator separation.

### Closest prior art

Czarnecki, Osindero, Jaderberg, Świrszcz, and Pascanu introduced derivative
supervision as *Sobolev Training for Neural Networks* (NeurIPS 2017),
[arXiv:1706.04859](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04859). Huge and Savine apply
the same principle to prices and pathwise Greeks in *Differential Machine
Learning* (2020), [arXiv:2005.02347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02347).
Nguwi, Penent, and Privault regress coding-tree labels with neural networks
in the JCP paper
[DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712).

The direct derivative-root label is a useful branching-specific source of
Sobolev targets, but the loss construction and the decomposition (5.3) are
not a novelty claim.

### Numerical falsification test

On an equation with exact \(u\), \(Du\), and \(D^2u\):

1. generate independent \(\mathrm{Id}\), \(D\), and \(D^2\) labels at the
   same training-state law;
2. verify that empirical estimates of
   \(\mathbb E[(Y_\mu-D^\mu u(X))h(X)]\) approach zero for several bounded
   test functions \(h\);
3. compare the measured label risk minus the derivative approximation error
   with the measured conditional-noise term in (5.3);
4. train value-only and derivative-supervised networks over many fixed seeds
   and evaluate both values and derivatives on a held-out grid;
5. report the empirical second moments and maximum label magnitudes before
   interpreting squared losses.

Failure of the orthogonality check falsifies the derivative label or the
state embedding, not Sobolev training in general.

### Quant-research relevance

One fitted surface can provide price/value estimates and internally
consistent Greeks. Direct tree labels avoid differentiating noisy Monte Carlo
sample means numerically. The main risk-control benefit is diagnostic:
derivative-label moments and denominator separation can be audited before a
network-generated hedge or HJB policy is reported.

## Candidate 6: Robust deep branching

### Precise statement

At a fixed training state, let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\) be independent real tree
labels with

\[
\mathbb EY_i=m,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)\leq\sigma^2<\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let \(n=KB\), where \(K\) is odd, split the observations into \(K\)
independent blocks of size \(B\), and let \(\widehat m_{\rm MOM}\) be the
median of their block means. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\!\left(
|\widehat m_{\rm MOM}-m|
>2\sigma\sqrt{\frac K n}
\right)
\leq e^{-K/8}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

For \(S\) fixed training states, a union bound gives simultaneous failure
probability at most \(S e^{-K/8}\), without requiring independence between
the statewise events.

This supplies a target-preserving robustification layer for per-state deep
branching labels under a finite second moment. A full robust-regression
theorem over a neural-network class would additionally require a robust risk
procedure and assumptions controlling that class; replacing labels by
statewise medians does not prove such a theorem.

### Minimal assumptions

1. Equation (6.2) requires independent labels with a common finite mean and
   a finite variance bounded by \(\sigma^2\).
2. A root-\(N\) Gaussian confidence interval also requires a finite second
   moment; a finite \((1+\epsilon)\)-moment with
   \(0<\epsilon<1\) supports slower robust-mean rates but not a
   finite-variance central limit theorem.
3. The statewise target must exist as a Lebesgue expectation. Robust
   estimation cannot assign a mean to a functional with both signed parts
   infinite.
4. For random-state regression, robust empirical-risk or
   median-of-means-tournament results require their own multiplier,
   small-ball, and hypothesis-class conditions. Nonconvex neural
   optimization adds an optimization gap not covered by (6.2).

### Status

**prior-art overlap**

### Proof or proof route

Let \(\bar Y_j\) be block \(j\)'s mean. Chebyshev's inequality gives

\[
\mathbb P\!\left(
|\bar Y_j-m|>\frac{2\sigma}{\sqrt B}
\right)
\leq
\frac{\sigma^2/B}{4\sigma^2/B}
=\frac14.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let \(I_j\) indicate this bad-block event. The block construction makes the
\(I_j\) independent and
\(\sum_j\mathbb EI_j\leq K/4\). If the median lies outside the interval
\([m-2\sigma/\sqrt B,m+2\sigma/\sqrt B]\), at least \(K/2\) blocks are bad.
Hoeffding's inequality therefore yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\!\left(
|\widehat m_{\rm MOM}-m|>\frac{2\sigma}{\sqrt B}
\right)
&\leq
\mathbb P\!\left(
\sum_{j=1}^KI_j-\sum_{j=1}^K\mathbb EI_j\geq\frac K4
\right)\\
&\leq
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2(K/4)^2}{K}\right)
=e^{-K/8}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Since \(B=n/K\), (6.4) is (6.2).

The target effects of alternative robustifications must be stated
separately.

* **Fixed clipping/truncation.** For
  \(T_\tau(y)=\operatorname{sgn}(y)\min(|y|,\tau)\), the clipped sample mean
  converges to
  \(m_\tau=\mathbb E[T_\tau(Y)]\), not generally to \(m\). Its asymptotic
  target bias is exactly
  \[
  b_\tau
  :=m_\tau-m
  =
  \mathbb E[
  (\tau\operatorname{sgn}Y-Y)\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>\tau\}}
  ].
  \tag{6.5}
  \]
  If \(\mathbb E|Y|^{1+\epsilon}\leq M\), then
  \[
  |b_\tau|
  \leq
  \mathbb E[|Y|\mathbf1_{\{|Y|>\tau\}}]
  \leq M\tau^{-\epsilon}.
  \tag{6.6}
  \]
  A sequence \(\tau_n\to\infty\) can make this bias vanish, but its growth
  must be balanced against sampling error.

* **Fixed-fraction trimming.** If \(F\) is continuous with integrable
  quantile function \(Q\), the two-sided \(\alpha\)-trimmed mean converges
  to
  \[
  m_\alpha
  =
  \frac1{1-2\alpha}
  \int_\alpha^{1-\alpha}Q(p)\,dp.
  \tag{6.7}
  \]
  Its asymptotic bias is \(m_\alpha-m\), which is generally nonzero for a
  skewed tree-label law and persists when \(\alpha\) is fixed.

* **The current percentile filter.** If its empirical \(1\%\) and \(99\%\)
  quantiles converge to \(Q_{.01}\) and \(Q_{.99}\), its multiplier \(c\)
  produces limiting thresholds
  \[
  L=Q_{.01}-c(Q_{.99}-Q_{.01}),\qquad
  U=Q_{.99}+c(Q_{.99}-Q_{.01}).
  \tag{6.8}
  \]
  Under integrability and no atoms at \(L,U\), the filtered mean converges
  to
  \[
  \frac{\mathbb E[Y\mathbf1_{\{L\leq Y\leq U\}}]}
       {\mathbb P(L\leq Y\leq U)},
  \tag{6.9}
  \]
  whose difference from \(m\) is its explicit asymptotic target bias.
  A large value of \(c\) can make the bias rare in finite samples without
  making it identically zero.

* **M-estimation.** For a fixed bounded odd score \(\psi\) and scale \(s\),
  a regular empirical M-estimator converges to the population root
  \[
  \theta_{\psi,s}:
  \quad
  \mathbb E\!\left[
  \psi\!\left(\frac{Y-\theta_{\psi,s}}s\right)
  \right]=0.
  \tag{6.10}
  \]
  Its asymptotic target bias for mean estimation is
  \(\theta_{\psi,s}-m\). It vanishes under suitable symmetry/Fisher
  consistency, but not for every skewed distribution. Catoni-type
  mean estimators use an \(n\)-dependent calibration to obtain deviations
  around \(m\) under a finite variance; they bound influence rather than
  deleting selected observations. They need not be exactly unbiased at
  finite \(n\).

Median-of-means also need not be exactly unbiased at finite \(n\), but with
block size \(B\to\infty\) it is consistent for \(m\) under the corresponding
moment assumptions. Its intended target is the original mean rather than a
fixed trimmed functional.

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

If \(\mathbb E|Y|=\infty\), there is no finite mean for a robust method to
recover. In the Dym case, both signed parts have infinite expectation, so
clipping can create a finite number only by changing the estimand.

Even when \(\mathbb EY\) exists but \(\mathbb EY^2=\infty\), (6.2), the
ordinary standard error, and squared-risk training guarantees based on a
variance are unavailable. A batch containing no extreme observation cannot
certify otherwise.

### Closest prior art

Lugosi and Mendelson survey robust mean and regression methods in *Mean
Estimation and Regression Under Heavy-Tailed Distributions* (2019),
[DOI 10.1007/s10208-019-09427-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-019-09427-x),
and develop robust risk minimization in *Risk minimization by
median-of-means tournaments*,
[DOI 10.4171/JEMS/937](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/937). Catoni's
finite-variance M-estimators appear in *Challenging the empirical mean and
empirical variance: A deviation study* (2012),
[DOI 10.1214/11-AIHP454](https://doi.org/10.1214/11-AIHP454).
Bias from trimming is studied, for example, by Danielak and Rychlik (2003),
[DOI 10.1111/1467-842X.00262](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-842X.00262).

The current deep-branching filter is documented in the implementation of
Nguwi, Penent, and Privault's method,
[DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712).
Huang and Privault's
[arXiv:2502.17853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17853) is relevant to checking
the moment premise before robust regression is attempted. The robust
statistics are established prior art; the branching-specific contribution
would be a transparent replacement and an estimator-target audit.

### Numerical falsification test

Use distributions and tree problems with known means:

1. a Gaussian control;
2. a skewed finite-variance mixture;
3. a finite-\((1+\epsilon)\)-moment Pareto-type control with infinite
   variance;
4. an integrable coding-tree benchmark with rare large observations; and
5. the non-integrable Dym functional as a negative control.

Across increasing sample sizes, compare the empirical mean,
median-of-means, Catoni-type estimation, fixed clipping, and the repository
percentile filter. Record estimates, block/tuning parameters, maximum
observation, deleted count, and coverage only where the required moment is
known. For fixed clipping and trimming, verify convergence toward (6.5) or
(6.7)/(6.9), rather than toward \(m\), on the skewed control. Any claimed
root-\(N\) coverage on the infinite-variance control falsifies the claimed
assumption set.

### Quant-research relevance

Robust mean and robust risk procedures protect training from finite batches
with heavy but integrable tails. The explicit target audit prevents a risk
system from obtaining smoother curves by silently deleting genuine scenario
mass. Confidence levels, moment requirements, and any target distortion can
be attached to each fitted surface.

## Candidate 7: Coupled multifidelity control variates

### Precise statement

Let \((H^\theta,H^0)\) be a real, square-integrable coupled pair with

\[
u^\theta=\mathbb EH^\theta,\qquad
u^0=\mathbb EH^0
\tag{7.1}
\]

and known \(u^0\). From \(N\) independent copies of the pair define

\[
\widehat u_\beta
=
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
\left[
H_i^\theta-\beta(H_i^0-u^0)
\right].
\tag{7.2}
\]

For every fixed \(\beta\), (7.2) is unbiased for \(u^\theta\). If
\(\operatorname{Var}(H^0)>0\), its variance is uniquely minimized by

\[
\boxed{
\beta^*
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(H^\theta,H^0)}
     {\operatorname{Var}(H^0)}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

If both variances are positive, then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat u_{\beta^*})
=
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(H^\theta)}{N}
\left(1-\operatorname{Corr}(H^\theta,H^0)^2\right).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The candidate branching-specific claim is stronger. For a parameter
perturbation of size \(\varepsilon=\|\theta-\theta_0\|\), seek a
common-tree coupling satisfying

\[
\left\|
(H^\theta-u^\theta)-(H^0-u^0)
\right\|_{L^2}
\leq C\varepsilon.
\tag{7.5}
\]

If (7.5) holds, then \(\beta=1\) and optimality imply

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat u_{\beta^*})
\leq
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat u_1)
\leq
\frac{C^2\varepsilon^2}{N}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

### Minimal assumptions

1. The pair is real and belongs to \(L^2\); (7.3) assumes
   \(\operatorname{Var}(H^0)>0\).
2. The expectation \(u^0\) is known exactly, or its estimation error and
   covariance are included in the variance calculation.
3. Paired samples are independent across \(i\), though the two components
   within each pair should be strongly coupled.
4. A data-estimated \(\beta\) is obtained on an independent pilot and then
   frozen, or its same-sample bias and variance are analyzed.
5. Claim (7.6) additionally requires the \(L^2\) coupling estimate (7.5)
   with a parameter-uniform constant on the stated neighborhood.

### Status

**conjecture**

### Proof or proof route

The algebraic control-variate result is complete. Centering does not change
covariance, and independence across pairs gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat u_\beta)
=
\frac1N
\left[
V_\theta-2\beta C_{\theta0}+\beta^2V_0
\right],
\tag{7.7}
\]

where

\[
V_\theta=\operatorname{Var}(H^\theta),\quad
V_0=\operatorname{Var}(H^0),\quad
C_{\theta0}=\operatorname{Cov}(H^\theta,H^0).
\tag{7.8}
\]

Completing the square,

\[
V_\theta-2\beta C_{\theta0}+\beta^2V_0
=
V_0\left(\beta-\frac{C_{\theta0}}{V_0}\right)^2
+V_\theta-\frac{C_{\theta0}^2}{V_0}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

This proves (7.3). Since

\[
\operatorname{Corr}(H^\theta,H^0)^2
=\frac{C_{\theta0}^2}{V_\theta V_0},
\tag{7.10}
\]

substitution into (7.9) proves (7.4). If \(V_0=0\), the control is constant
almost surely and every \(\beta\) has the same variance; if \(V_\theta=0\),
the high-fidelity estimator already has zero variance.

For the conjectural coupling, use the same exponential clocks, uniforms for
labelled tuple choices, Brownian marks, and child labels wherever the two
models have common support. Proving (7.5) would require a coupled
code-indexed second-moment recursion and an \(L^2\) contraction or
perturbation bound. Once (7.5) is available,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H^\theta-H^0)
=
\left\|
(H^\theta-u^\theta)-(H^0-u^0)
\right\|_2^2
\leq C^2\varepsilon^2,
\tag{7.11}
\]

and (7.6) follows from (7.7) at \(\beta=1\) and the minimizing property of
\(\beta^*\).

The \(O(\varepsilon^2/N)\) statement remains a conjecture until (7.5), or an
equivalent coupled \(L^2\) estimate, is proved for the target branching
model.

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

A deterministic low-fidelity approximation alone is not a random control:
if \(H^0=u^0\) almost surely, then \(V_0=0\) and (7.2) is unchanged.
Likewise, independently simulating \(H^\theta\) and \(H^0\) gives zero
covariance and no variance reduction.

Closeness of PDE solutions does not imply (7.5). A perturbation can alter a
mechanism support, expose an inverse likelihood with small probability, or
move a terminal singularity. Then \(u^\theta-u^0=O(\varepsilon)\) may coexist
with an \(L^2\) difference of order one or infinity.

### Closest prior art

Control variates and (7.3)--(7.4) are classical; see Glasserman,
*Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering*,
[DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-21617-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-21617-1).
Multifidelity allocation and unbiased control-variate estimators are treated
by Peherstorfer, Willcox, and Gunzburger in *Optimal Model Management for
Multifidelity Monte Carlo Estimation*,
[DOI 10.1137/15M1046472](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1046472), and their
survey,
[DOI 10.1137/16M1082469](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469).

The high- and low-fidelity branching representations are closest to Nguwi,
Penent, and Privault,
[DOI 10.1007/s00028-023-00873-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00028-023-00873-3),
and Henry-Labordère et al.,
[DOI 10.1214/17-AIHP880](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-AIHP880). The unresolved
part is not the control-variate algebra; it is an \(L^2\)-stable common-tree
coupling for the parameterized arbitrary-jet mechanism.

### Numerical falsification test

For perturbations \(\varepsilon_j\downarrow0\):

1. generate paired trees from shared primitive random numbers and, as a
   negative control, independently generated trees;
2. estimate
   \(\mathbb E| (H^\theta-u^\theta)-(H^0-u^0)|^2\) only after both
   component second moments are certified;
3. plot that quantity against \(\varepsilon\) on logarithmic axes and test
   whether the slope is two;
4. estimate \(\beta\) on a pilot, freeze it, and evaluate means, correlation,
   variance, and variance times mean node cost on fresh samples;
5. inspect mismatched topologies and maximum likelihood ratios separately.

A slope below two, a nonvanishing centered-difference variance, or a
diverging second moment falsifies (7.5) on the tested family.

### Quant-research relevance

Reduced and full stochastic-rate HJB models naturally form a fidelity pair.
A successful coupling would accelerate parameter sweeps, calibration
scenarios, and stress testing while retaining an unbiased high-fidelity
target. The coupling proof is also a model-risk control: it identifies when
a cheap approximation is genuinely informative path by path rather than only
close in mean.

## Candidate 8: Coefficient sensitivities and policy intervals

### Precise statement

Use a coefficient-labelled finite tree or killed-depth tree whose proposal
law is fixed independently of a vector \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_R)\). Suppose each
occurrence of label \(r\) contributes one multiplicative factor \(a_r\).
For a realized tree,

\[
H(a,\omega)
=R(\omega)\prod_{s=1}^R a_s^{N_s(\omega)},
\tag{8.1}
\]

where \(N_s\) is the finite number of occurrences of coefficient \(a_s\) and
\(R\) is independent of \(a\). For \(a_r\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{
\partial_{a_r}H(a,\omega)
=H(a,\omega)\frac{N_r(\omega)}{a_r}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Let \(I\) be a compact interval around \(a_r\) that excludes zero. If

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\sup_{s\in I}
\left|H(a_1,\ldots,s,\ldots,a_R)\right|
\frac{N_r}{|s|}
\right]<\infty,
\tag{8.3}
\]

then \(u(a)=\mathbb E[H(a)]\) is differentiable in \(a_r\) and

\[
\boxed{
\partial_{a_r}u(a)
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
H(a)\frac{N_r}{a_r}
\right].
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

For a full coding tree, (8.4) additionally requires that its expectation
represents the PDE solution and that (8.3), or another valid Leibniz
condition, holds for the unrestricted functional.

### Minimal assumptions

1. The mechanism is expanded into coefficient-labelled monomial terms so
   that (8.1) is true. A coefficient hidden additively inside an unevaluated
   \(f\)-derivative does not admit the count formula.
2. The topology, lifetime, Markov, and tuple proposal laws are fixed as
   \(a_r\) varies, with a common full support.
3. The realized tree and \(N_r\) are finite almost surely.
4. The coefficient \(a_r\) is nonzero and varies in an interval separated
   from zero.
5. The domination (8.3) licenses differentiation under the expectation.
6. For a joint central limit theorem for values, direct jets, and the score
   \(HN_r/a_r\), their joint second moment is finite. For unrestricted trees,
   \(H\in L^2\) alone does not imply \(HN_r\in L^2\).
7. A reported policy map is continuously differentiable at the target jet,
   and each denominator is bounded away from zero on the reporting domain.

### Status

**conditional theorem**

### Proof or proof route

For a fixed realization, all factors other than \(a_r^{N_r}\) are constant
with respect to \(a_r\). Since \(N_r\) is a nonnegative integer,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{a_r}H(a)
&=
R\!\left(\prod_{s\ne r}a_s^{N_s}\right)
N_r a_r^{N_r-1}\\
&=
H(a)\frac{N_r}{a_r},
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]

which proves (8.2), including the case \(N_r=0\).

For \(h\) small enough that \(a_r+h\in I\), the one-dimensional mean-value
theorem applied to (8.1) gives a random
\(\xi_h\) between \(a_r\) and \(a_r+h\) such that

\[
\frac{H(a+he_r)-H(a)}h
=
H(a_1,\ldots,\xi_h,\ldots,a_R)\frac{N_r}{\xi_h}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

The absolute value of (8.6) is bounded by the integrable variable in (8.3),
and it converges pointwise to (8.2). Dominated convergence therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{a_r}\mathbb E[H(a)]
&=
\lim_{h\to0}
\mathbb E\!\left[
\frac{H(a+he_r)-H(a)}h
\right]\\
&=
\mathbb E\!\left[
H(a)\frac{N_r}{a_r}
\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.7}
\]

proving (8.4).

For policy intervals, collect all required unbiased tree variables into
\(Z\in\mathbb R^p\), including direct jets and coefficient scores. If
\(\mathbb E\|Z\|^2<\infty\), then

\[
\sqrt N(\bar Z_N-\mathbb EZ)
\ \Longrightarrow\
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma).
\tag{8.8}
\]

For a differentiable policy \(G\) whose denominators are separated from
zero, the multivariate delta method yields

\[
\sqrt N\bigl(G(\bar Z_N)-G(\mathbb EZ)\bigr)
\ \Longrightarrow\
\mathcal N\!\left(
0,\nabla G^\top\Sigma\nabla G
\right).
\tag{8.9}
\]

A plug-in covariance gives pointwise asymptotic intervals. Simultaneous
state-domain bands require an additional functional limit theorem or a
multiple-testing construction.

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

At \(a_r=0\), the right side of (8.4) is undefined. The derivative may still
exist: in a monomial expansion it receives contributions from trees with
exactly one \(r\)-occurrence. Recovering it requires a zero-safe expansion,
not division by \(a_r\).

The identity also fails as written if \(a_r\) changes the sampling law or
mechanism support, or if it appears inside an additive factor such as
\(a_rB+C\). In those cases the derivative has proposal-score or
factor-specific terms. Finally, a policy denominator with positive density
near zero can make ratio moments infinite even when every input estimator
has a finite variance.

### Closest prior art

Likelihood-ratio and pathwise sensitivity methods are classical simulation
tools; see Reiman and Weiss, *Sensitivity Analysis for Simulations via
Likelihood Ratios* (1989),
[DOI 10.1287/opre.37.5.830](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.37.5.830), and
Glasserman,
[DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-21617-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-21617-1).
For structural parameters, see Peng, Fu, Hu, and Heidergott, *A New Unbiased
Stochastic Derivative Estimator for Discontinuous Sample Performances with
Structural Parameters*,
[DOI 10.1287/opre.2017.1674](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2017.1674).

Nguwi, Penent, and Privault supply the coefficient-bearing coding trees,
[DOI 10.1007/s00028-023-00873-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00028-023-00873-3),
while Huge and Savine supply the financial differential-learning context,
[arXiv:2005.02347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02347). Formula (8.4) is a
simple pathwise product derivative specialized to a coefficient-labelled
tree; no general sensitivity-method novelty is claimed.

### Numerical falsification test

On a finite-depth polynomial control:

1. record \(H\) and every \(N_r\) on each path;
2. compare the sample mean of \(HN_r/a_r\) with an exact analytic
   derivative and with common-random-number finite differences over a
   decreasing step ladder;
3. verify the identity path by path before testing expectations;
4. approach \(a_r=0\) to expose the division singularity and compare with
   the exactly-one-occurrence expansion;
5. for policy intervals, use a benchmark with exact jets, check empirical
   covariance and repeated-sample coverage, and report the minimum observed
   policy denominator.

A finite-difference agreement without the pathwise check would not
distinguish (8.4) from accidental cancellation.

### Quant-research relevance

The count score gives coefficient Greeks for calibration, parameter stress,
and local model-risk attribution without rerunning every perturbed model.
Combined direct-jet and coefficient-score samples preserve covariance needed
for policy uncertainty. The denominator and second-moment checks prevent
unstable controls from being presented with misleading Gaussian intervals.

## Candidate 9: Monte Carlo challenger-model certificate

### Precise statement

Let \((\mathcal V,\|\cdot\|)\) be a normed vector space, let
\(v_1,\ldots,v_m\in\mathcal V\) be deterministic candidate solver outputs,
and let \(u\in\mathcal V\) be the target solution. Given a random reference
\(\widehat u\), choose any measurable tie-breaking rule and set

\[
\widehat\ell\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\leq\ell\leq m}
\|v_\ell-\widehat u\|.
\tag{9.1}
\]

On the event

\[
\|\widehat u-u\|\leq r,
\tag{9.2}
\]

one has the finite-candidate oracle inequality

\[
\boxed{
\|v_{\widehat\ell}-u\|
\leq
\min_{1\leq\ell\leq m}\|v_\ell-u\|+2r.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

If a valid robust Monte Carlo construction makes (9.2) hold with probability
at least \(1-\delta\), then (9.3) holds with at least the same probability.

### Minimal assumptions

1. There are finitely many candidates, so the minimum in (9.1) is attained.
2. All candidates, the target, and the reference use the same state
   embedding, grid/quadrature rule, and norm.
3. The radius \(r\) certifies the entire norm in (9.2). Pointwise error bars
   do not automatically certify a continuum \(L^2\) or supremum norm.
4. Any probabilistic claim for (9.2) uses a moment assumption valid for the
   Monte Carlo functional. A Gaussian radius requires \(L^2\); a robust
   finite-variance radius must use its stated confidence construction.
5. The certificate is against the represented target \(u\). Bias from depth
   truncation, filtering, or an invalid representation must be added to
   \(r\).

### Status

**proved theorem**

### Proof or proof route

Let

\[
\ell^*\in
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\leq\ell\leq m}\|v_\ell-u\|.
\tag{9.4}
\]

On (9.2), the triangle inequality, the minimizing property of
\(\widehat\ell\), and the triangle inequality again give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|v_{\widehat\ell}-u\|
&\leq
\|v_{\widehat\ell}-\widehat u\|
+\|\widehat u-u\|\\
&\leq
\|v_{\ell^*}-\widehat u\|+r\\
&\leq
\|v_{\ell^*}-u\|
+\|u-\widehat u\|+r\\
&\leq
\min_\ell\|v_\ell-u\|+2r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.5}
\]

This proves (9.3). The probability statement follows by event inclusion:
the event (9.2) is a subset of the event on which (9.3) holds.

The constant two is sharp without a margin assumption. On the real line,
take \(u=0\), \(\widehat u=r\), \(v_1=0\), and
\(v_2=2r-\eta\) with \(0<\eta<r\). Candidate \(v_2\) is uniquely closer to
\(\widehat u\), yet

\[
|v_2-u|=2r-\eta
=\min_\ell|v_\ell-u|+2r-\eta.
\tag{9.6}
\]

Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) rules out a universal remainder smaller than
\(2r\).

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

If the branching functional has no mean, the reference cannot certify the
PDE target. If it has a mean but no second moment, a sample standard-error
radius does not justify (9.2). The geometric inequality remains true, but
its probabilistic premise is absent.

The certificate also cannot distinguish candidates whose target errors differ
by less than the uncertainty scale. Example (9.6) shows that a noisy nearest
candidate can be almost \(2r\) worse than the oracle candidate.

### Closest prior art

The consistency plot in Nguwi, Penent, and Privault's deep branching solver,
[DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2023.112712),
compares learned outputs with branching labels, but does not state (9.3).
Robust reference construction belongs to the heavy-tailed mean/regression
literature surveyed by Lugosi and Mendelson,
[DOI 10.1007/s10208-019-09427-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-019-09427-x).
The underlying selection proof is a two-use triangle inequality and belongs
to the general oracle-inequality pattern; the theorem is useful here as an
auditable validation rule rather than as a broad novelty claim.

### Numerical falsification test

1. Use a benchmark with an exact grid solution \(u\).
2. Construct an independent Monte Carlo reference and a radius whose
   repeated-sample coverage of (9.2) is measured.
3. Compare deterministic FD, BSDE, Galerkin, and neural candidates in one
   predeclared norm.
4. For every replication satisfying (9.2), assert (9.3) directly.
5. Include the one-dimensional construction (9.6) as a unit test for the
   factor two and tie-breaking edge cases.

Any violation of (9.3) while (9.2) is numerically verified indicates a norm,
alignment, minimization, or implementation error.

### Quant-research relevance

The result turns an independent stochastic solver into a challenger-model
selection certificate with a transparent uncertainty penalty. It is useful
for model validation because it separates candidate approximation error from
reference uncertainty, while refusing to treat Monte Carlo as an infallible
benchmark.

## Candidate 10: Short-time wave proposal optimization

### Precise statement

Consider the one-dimensional nonlinear wave equation

\[
u_{tt}-c^2u_{xx}=f(u),\qquad
f(y)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}a_k y^k,
\qquad
u(0,z)=\phi(z),\quad u_t(0,z)=\psi(z),
\tag{10.1}
\]

where \(\mathcal K\) is a finite nonempty set and every displayed \(a_k\ne0\).
The coefficients, data, wave speed, and state may be complex; variance means
the quadratic risk \(\mathbb E|H-\mathbb EH|^2\). Let

\[
b:=\phi(z)\ne0,\qquad
A_b:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}|a_k|\,|b|^k,
\tag{10.2}
\]

and let \(q_k>0\), \(\sum_kq_k=1\), be the offspring proposal. For horizon
\(t\), use an exponential clock with the global tree rate

\[
\lambda_t=\gamma t,\qquad \gamma>0.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Let \(H_t\) be the d'Alembert branching estimator

\[
H_t=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
e^{\lambda_t t}
\left[
\frac{\phi(z+ct)+\phi(z-ct)}2
+t\psi(z+ctU)
\right],
&\tau\geq t,\\[3mm]
\displaystyle
e^{\lambda_t\tau}
\frac{\tau}{\lambda_t}\frac{a_K}{q_K}
\prod_{j=1}^{K}
H_{t-\tau}^{(j)}(z+c\tau U),
&\tau<t,
\end{cases}
\tag{10.4}
\]

where \(U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[-1,1]\), \(K\) has law \(q\), and the child
continuations are independent conditional on their shared birth state.
The empty product for \(K=0\) is one.

Under the assumptions below, as \(t\downarrow0\),

\[
\mathbb EH_t
=
b+\psi(z)t
+\frac12\bigl(c^2\phi''(z)+f(b)\bigr)t^2
+o(t^2),
\tag{10.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(H_t)
=
t^2
\left[
\gamma|b|^2
+\frac1{3\gamma}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
\frac{|a_k|^2|b|^{2k}}{q_k}
-\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\overline b\,f(b)\right)
\right]
+o(t^2).
}
\tag{10.6}
\]

The proposal-dependent leading coefficient is jointly and uniquely minimized
by

\[
\boxed{
q_k^*
=
\frac{|a_k|\,|b|^k}{A_b},
\qquad
\gamma^*
=
\frac{A_b}{\sqrt3\,|b|},
\qquad
\lambda_t^*
=
\frac{A_b}{\sqrt3\,|b|}\,t.
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

At this proposal,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H_t)
=
t^2
\left[
\frac{2|b|A_b}{\sqrt3}
-\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\overline b\,f(b)\right)
\right]
+o(t^2).
\tag{10.8}
\]

### Minimal assumptions

1. The live polynomial support \(\mathcal K\) is finite and nonempty,
   \(a_k\ne0\), and \(q_k>0\).
2. The local value \(b=\phi(z)\) is nonzero.
3. The functions \(\phi\) and \(\psi\) have, respectively, two and one
   continuous derivatives on a neighborhood of the short complex cone
   reached from \(z\). For complex \(c,z\), the corresponding directional
   Taylor expansions must hold, for example through holomorphic extensions.
4. These functions are locally bounded there. Finite offspring arity,
   the positive lower bound on the fixed \(q\), and sufficiently small
   \(t\) then give a uniform second-moment bound for the short tree.
5. Every child uses the same global rate \(\lambda_t=\gamma t\), as in the
   reference recursion, rather than recomputing a new rate from its remaining
   horizon.

### Status

**proved theorem**

### Proof or proof route

Put

\[
L_t(U)
:=
\frac{\phi(z+ct)+\phi(z-ct)}2
+t\psi(z+ctU).
\tag{10.9}
\]

Uniformly in \(U\in[-1,1]\), Taylor expansion gives

\[
L_t(U)
=
b+\psi(z)t
+\left[
\frac{c^2}{2}\phi''(z)+c\psi'(z)U
\right]t^2
+o(t^2).
\tag{10.10}
\]

Since \(\mathbb EU=0\),

\[
\mathbb EL_t
=b+\psi(z)t+\frac{c^2}{2}\phi''(z)t^2+o(t^2),
\tag{10.11}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|L_t|^2
={}&
|b|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline b\,\psi(z))t\\
&+
\left[
|\psi(z)|^2
+\operatorname{Re}(\overline b\,c^2\phi''(z))
\right]t^2
+o(t^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.12}
\]

The random \(U\)-term in (10.10) first contributes to the leaf variance at
order \(t^4\), so it does not appear in (10.12).

The probability density of a first branch at \(s<t\) is
\(\lambda_te^{-\lambda_ts}\). On the leaf event, the density and likelihood
cancel for the first moment:

\[
\mathbb E[H_t;\tau\geq t]
=e^{-\lambda_tt}e^{\lambda_tt}\mathbb EL_t
=\mathbb EL_t.
\tag{10.13}
\]

For a branch, tuple probability, clock density, and likelihood also cancel:

\[
\mathbb E[H_t;\tau<t]
=
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}a_k
\int_0^t
s\,
\mathbb E\!\left[
\prod_{j=1}^k
H_{t-s}^{(j)}(z+csU)
\right]ds.
\tag{10.14}
\]

The short-tree second moments are uniformly bounded for small \(t\). One
way to see this is to expand by the number of branch events. Each particle
branches with probability at most
\(\lambda_tt=\gamma t^2\), every branch has at most
\(\max\mathcal K\) children, and its magnitude multiplier satisfies

\[
e^{\lambda_ts}\frac{s}{\lambda_t}
\left|\frac{a_k}{q_k}\right|
\leq
e^{\gamma t^2}
\frac1\gamma
\max_{j\in\mathcal K}\frac{|a_j|}{q_j}.
\tag{10.15}
\]

The number of labelled finite-arity topologies with \(n\) branch events
grows at most exponentially in \(n\). For small enough \(t\), the resulting
nonnegative second-moment series is dominated by a convergent geometric
series. Topologies with at least one branch have total second-moment weight
\(O(t^2)\), and those with at least two have weight \(O(t^4)\).
Consequently, uniformly for \(0\leq s\leq t\), the child continuation from
\(z+csU\) converges to \(b\) in \(L^2\). Conditional child independence then
gives

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\prod_{j=1}^kH_{t-s}^{(j)}(z+csU)
\right]
=b^k+o(1).
\tag{10.16}
\]

Substituting (10.16) into (10.14), changing variables \(s=tr\), and using
the finiteness of \(\mathcal K\) yields

\[
\mathbb E[H_t;\tau<t]
=
\frac{t^2}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}a_kb^k+o(t^2)
=\frac{t^2}{2}f(b)+o(t^2).
\tag{10.17}
\]

Equations (10.11), (10.13), and (10.17) prove (10.5).

For the second moment, the leaf contribution is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[|H_t|^2;\tau\geq t]
&=
e^{-\lambda_tt}e^{2\lambda_tt}
\mathbb E|L_t|^2\\
&=
e^{\gamma t^2}\mathbb E|L_t|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.18}
\]

Using (10.12),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[|H_t|^2;\tau\geq t]
={}&
|b|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline b\,\psi(z))t\\
&+
\left[
|\psi(z)|^2
+\operatorname{Re}(\overline b\,c^2\phi''(z))
+\gamma|b|^2
\right]t^2
+o(t^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.19}
\]

For a first branch, conditional child independence after conditioning on the
shared birth state gives the exact nonnegative expression

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[|H_t|^2;\tau<t]
={}&
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
\frac{|a_k|^2}{q_k}
\int_0^t
e^{\lambda_ts}\frac{s^2}{\lambda_t}\\
&\quad\times
\mathbb E_U\!\left[
\prod_{j=1}^k
\mathbb E\!\left(
|H_{t-s}^{(j)}(z+csU)|^2
\mid U,s
\right)
\right]ds.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.20}
\]

The product in (10.20) converges uniformly to \(|b|^{2k}\). Since
\(\lambda_t=\gamma t\), the change \(s=tr\) gives

\[
\frac1{\lambda_t}\int_0^t e^{\lambda_ts}s^2\,ds
=
\frac{t^2}{\gamma}
\int_0^1e^{\gamma t^2r}r^2\,dr
=
\frac{t^2}{3\gamma}+o(t^2).
\tag{10.21}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[|H_t|^2;\tau<t]
=
\frac{t^2}{3\gamma}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
\frac{|a_k|^2|b|^{2k}}{q_k}
+o(t^2).
\tag{10.22}
\]

Squaring (10.5) in modulus gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathbb EH_t|^2
={}&
|b|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline b\,\psi(z))t\\
&+
\left[
|\psi(z)|^2+
\operatorname{Re}\!\left(
\overline b(c^2\phi''(z)+f(b))
\right)
\right]t^2
+o(t^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.23}
\]

Adding (10.19) and (10.22), then subtracting (10.23), proves (10.6).

It remains to optimize. Put

\[
r_k:=|a_k|\,|b|^k>0,\qquad
S(q):=\sum_k\frac{r_k^2}{q_k}.
\tag{10.24}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
S(q)\geq\left(\sum_kr_k\right)^2=A_b^2,
\tag{10.25}
\]

with equality exactly at \(q_k=r_k/A_b\). For fixed \(q\),

\[
\gamma|b|^2+\frac{S(q)}{3\gamma}
\geq
\frac{2|b|\sqrt{S(q)}}{\sqrt3},
\tag{10.26}
\]

with equality exactly at
\(\gamma=\sqrt{S(q)}/(\sqrt3|b|)\). Combining (10.25)--(10.26) proves
(10.7)--(10.8). The term
\(-\operatorname{Re}(\overline b f(b))\) is proposal independent.

The scaling \(\lambda_t\asymp t\) itself balances two effects. The leaf
likelihood contributes \(\lambda_t t\,|b|^2\) to leading order, while the
branch second moment contributes
\(t^3S(q)/(3\lambda_t)\). Rates with
\(\lambda_t/t\to0\) or \(\lambda_t/t\to\infty\) cannot achieve the balanced
order \(t^2\) while \(\lambda_tt\to0\).

### Decisive obstruction/counterexample

The formula does not cover \(\phi(z)=b=0\). Then the weights
\(|a_k||b|^k\) vanish for every \(k>0\), \(\gamma^*\) divides by zero, and
the leading leaf scale may be \(t\psi(z)\) rather than a nonzero constant.
If \(a_0\ne0\), a constant-offspring branch can dominate at a different
scale; if \(a_0=0\), the first live power and the vanishing order of the data
determine the balance. There is no single continuation of (10.7) through
\(b=0\).

The result is also local. At a longer horizon, descendant branching,
terminal growth, and computational cost can reverse the ranking supplied by
the \(t^2\) coefficient.

### Closest prior art

Chan and Privault give the cone-based estimator (10.4), its integrability
conditions, and wave-equation experiments in *Probabilistic representation
and classical solutions of wave equations with complex polynomial
nonlinearities* (2026),
[arXiv:2606.18919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18919). Henry-Labordère and
Touzi treat branching representations for nonlinear Cauchy problems,
including wave equations, in *Branching diffusion representation for
nonlinear Cauchy problems and Monte Carlo approximation* (2021),
[DOI 10.1214/20-AAP1649](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AAP1649).

The square-root proposal principle overlaps the local optimizer in the
companion `adaptive-proposals.md` and classical importance sampling. The
specific joint asymptotic formula (10.6)--(10.7) for the d'Alembert
estimator has not been assigned priority by a comprehensive literature
review.

### Numerical falsification test

Choose analytic \(\phi,\psi\), a nonzero \(b=\phi(z)\), and a finite
polynomial \(f\). For a decreasing horizon ladder:

1. set \(\lambda=\gamma t\) and estimate
   \(\operatorname{Var}(H_t)/t^2\) without filtering;
2. compare it with the bracket in (10.6) over a grid of \((q,\gamma)\);
3. increase the sample budget proportionally to at least \(t^{-2}\), or use
   deterministic first-event quadrature, so the \(O(t^2)\) branch event is
   observed;
4. verify that fitted minimizers approach (10.7);
5. repeat at a point with \(\phi(z)=0\) as a negative control and check that
   the scaling changes.

A persistent nonzero limit after subtracting (10.6), or a minimizing
\((q,\gamma)\) away from (10.7) as \(t\downarrow0\), falsifies the expansion
under the tested implementation.

### Quant-research relevance

The calculation is a compact example of proposal design from asymptotic
second moments rather than ad hoc rate tuning. It separates estimator
variance, likelihood weighting, and offspring allocation, and it shows why a
locally optimal clock can scale with maturity. The same balance appears in
short-dated nonlinear valuation and stress calculations, although the
wave-equation result is supporting work from `wavelab`, not a parabolic
finance benchmark.
